**What it is:** PFAS (Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances) are a family of synthetic chemicals used in non-stick coatings, grease-resistant packaging, and water-repellent treatments. Several states have banned or restricted them in cookware and food-contact products.

- **Waxed canvas (like your apron):** The wax treatment may or may not involve PFAS — ask your manufacturer specifically.

## 2. Cookware Disclosure

**What it is:** Required by some state laws (notably California AB 1200) and retailer policies. It requires you to disclose what materials the cooking surface is made of, and flag if any restricted substances are present.

**What the field asks:** Typically: what is the cooking surface material, and does it contain any listed chemicals?

**How to answer it:**
- **Cast iron / enameled cast iron:** Disclose the enamel coating material. Enameled cast iron is generally safe — no special chemical disclosures typically needed.
- **Non-stick:** You must disclose PTFE, PFOA status, and any other coating chemicals.
